# Data ethics

Forest and biodiversity data can carry ecological, geographic, licensing, and personal risks even when collected for legitimate research.

## Principles

- Use only public or explicitly reusable data.
- Minimize collection, copying, and retention.
- Do not publish personal or sensitive information.
- Treat precise locations of vulnerable species, nests, habitats, and protected sites as potentially sensitive.
- Prefer aggregation or spatial generalization when exact coordinates are not required.
- Do not infer ecological conclusions beyond what a dataset and validation protocol support.
- Keep provenance, licensing, and scientific-claim boundaries visible.
- Distinguish source measurements and labels from model predictions or derived indicators.

## Location sensitivity

Before publishing real observations, assess whether coordinates could enable disturbance, collection, poaching, trespass, or other harm. When in doubt, do not publish exact locations.

The Kartverket views in this prototype provide regional Lillomarka context. They are not presented as exact coordinates of the selected FOR-age trees.

## AI outputs

Model predictions are candidate interpretations, not ground truth. Any future public empirical output should state its validation basis, uncertainty, model/version context, and data origin.
