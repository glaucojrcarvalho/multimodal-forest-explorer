# Data ethics

Forest and biodiversity data can carry ecological, geographic, and personal risks even when collected for legitimate research.

## Principles

- Use only public, synthetic, or explicitly licensed data.
- Minimize collection and retention.
- Do not publish personal or sensitive information.
- Treat precise locations of vulnerable species, nests, habitats, and protected sites as potentially sensitive.
- Prefer aggregation or spatial generalization when exact coordinates are not required.
- Do not infer ecological conclusions beyond what a dataset and validation protocol support.
- Keep provenance and uncertainty visible.
- Separate demonstration data from scientific evidence.

## Location sensitivity

Before publishing real observations, assess whether coordinates could enable disturbance, collection, poaching, trespass, or other harm. When in doubt, do not publish exact locations.

## AI outputs

Model predictions are candidate interpretations, not ground truth. Public-facing outputs should state their validation basis, uncertainty, and data origin.
